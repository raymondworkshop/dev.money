# [How to Ask for Help from People Who Don't Know You](https://pradyuprasad.com/writings/how-to-ask-for-help/)

## Core View
- The key to asking for help is to put yourself in the other person’s mind, not at the front of the request.
- Help is about people, not projects—focus on demonstrating you are a serious, credible individual through proof of work (e.g., a blog post, trained model, vlog).
- Personal connections (e.g., 'Steve suggested I reach out') can help, but risk damaging credibility if the third party is disliked or untrustworthy.
- Institutional credibility (e.g., being a student at a prestigious university) is weak and should be used sparingly.
- Before asking for help, explain context briefly and connect it to the other person’s known interests or expertise.
- Make the request easy to accept by reducing friction: be specific, low-magnitude, and bounded (e.g., ask for a 10-minute review, not a full manuscript).
- Make it easy to say no—avoid guilt, pressure, or pestering. A polite, grateful 'no' preserves the relationship and encourages future help.

## Key Takeaways
- Asking for help is a skill, not a trait—success depends on empathy and understanding the recipient’s perspective.
- Credibility is built through proof of work, not just status or connections.
- Context should be concise and relevant to the recipient’s existing knowledge or interests.
- Low-friction, bounded requests increase acceptance rates and preserve long-term relationships.
