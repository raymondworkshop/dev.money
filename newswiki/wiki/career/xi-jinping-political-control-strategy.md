# [Xi Jinping's Use of Stalin and Mao's Strategies to Suppress Dissent](https://cn.wsj.com/articles/%E4%B9%A0%E8%BF%91%E5%B9%B3%E5%A6%82%E4%BD%95%E8%BF%90%E7%94%A8%E6%96%AF%E5%A4%A7%E6%9E%97%E5%92%8C%E6%AF%9B%E6%B3%BD%E4%B8%9C%E7%9A%84%E7%AD%96%E7%95%A5%E5%BC%BA%E5%8A%9B%E5%8E%8B%E5%88%B6%E5%BC%82%E8%A7%81-67a1675d?mod=cn_hp_mediumtopper_pos_1)

## Centralized Power and Purges
- Xi Jinping has conducted a series of high-level purges, including the removal of three Politburo members within six months, the highest level since 1976.
- Senior officials including the Defense Minister, Foreign Minister, and Agriculture Minister have been dismissed, along with military commanders and financial regulators.
- Officials are required to undergo criticism and self-criticism sessions, evaluated on loyalty to Xi Jinping's leadership.

## Erosion of Democratic Processes
- Xi has abolished term limits for the presidency and removed age-based retirement rules, enabling indefinite rule.
- The Five-Year Plans are now developed without input from external experts like the World Bank or Joseph Stiglitz.
- Feedback from government institutions, retired officials, and external analysts is systematically reduced or ignored.

## Institutional Control and Surveillance
- A vast internal rule system has been established, with the Central Commission for Discipline Inspection (CCDI) empowered to monitor and investigate over 100 million party members.
- Xi promotes a culture of mutual reporting and internal surveillance, though without the mass terror seen under Stalin.
- The party has institutionalized the practice of 'mutual supervision' and centralized oversight through national inspection teams.

## Personal Cult and Ideological Legitimization
- Xi is portrayed as the 'core leader' of China's revival, with his policies tied to the 'Xi Jinping Thought on Socialism with Chinese Characteristics for a New Era'.
- This ideology is enshrined in the Party Constitution and the State Constitution, and all new party members are required to study it.
- Official media frequently refers to Xi as the 'People's Leader', echoing Mao's 'Great Leader' title to legitimize centralized authority.

## Succession and Risk of Instability
- No clear successor has been identified, and no younger, experienced official has been promoted to the Politburo.
- Historical precedent shows that both Stalin and Mao faced political instability after death, with power struggles and policy reversals following.
- [AI Synthesis] This lack of a clear succession plan introduces significant risk of institutional collapse or civil unrest if Xi's leadership ends unexpectedly.

## Key Takeaways
- Xi Jinping is using centralized, authoritarian strategies reminiscent of Stalin and Mao to consolidate power and suppress dissent.
- The absence of a clear successor and the erosion of institutional checks raise concerns about long-term political stability.
- While the methods differ in scale and brutality, the underlying mechanisms—purges, personal loyalty, and ideological control—are consistent with historical patterns of authoritarian rule.
