# [How a $33/Hour Costco Cashier Built a Million-Dollar Retirement: A Case Study in Employee Retention and Well-being](https://cn.wsj.com/articles/%E6%97%B6%E8%96%AA33%E7%BE%8E%E5%85%83%E7%9A%84%E5%BC%80%E5%B8%82%E5%AE%A2%E6%94%B6%E9%93%B6%E5%91%98-%E5%A6%82%E4%BD%95%E6%94%92%E5%87%BA%E7%99%BE%E4%B8%87%E8%BA%AB%E5%AE%B6-4223869c?mod=cn_hp_lead_pos10)

## Core View
- Tony Barzar, a 60-year-old cashier with 40 years of service at Costco, earns a base hourly wage of $32.90, one of the highest in the retail sector.
- His retirement savings exceed $100,000, largely due to the company's 401(k) plan managed by T. Rowe Price, with consistent contributions over decades.
- Costco provides comprehensive healthcare, including $15 for routine visits and $25 for specialist visits, significantly below the national average.
- The company retains experienced employees through cultural coaching, higher wages, and additional paid time off, reducing turnover and preserving institutional knowledge.
- Costco's employee retention strategy results in a 7% annual turnover rate—far below industry average—and reduces training costs and customer dissatisfaction.

## Employee Retention and Cultural Impact
- Costco treats experienced employees as cultural ambassadors, assigning them as 'culture coaches' to mentor new hires and preserve brand values.
- The company's policy of not promoting long-serving employees to management roles emphasizes their value in frontline service and customer interaction.
- A 2023 McKinsey study found that employees with high job satisfaction are twice as likely to have customers with high satisfaction scores.
- For every frontline employee lost, the company estimates a $10,000 loss in productivity and service quality.
- Costco's approach reduces the risk of cultural dilution when new employees join, as experienced staff ensure consistent service standards.

## Personal and Professional Motivations
- Barzar initially joined Costco in 1986 as a parking attendant, later transitioning to a cashier role after a personal interest in customer service and community engagement.
- He declined opportunities to become a firefighter, citing a sense of belonging and long-term stability in his retail career.
- Personal hardships—loss of his son-in-law and a wife diagnosed with stage III brain cancer—highlighted the value of Costco's healthcare and mental health support, including paid leave and counseling services.
- His work includes direct customer interaction, such as helping customers with large purchases, offering emotional support, and resolving minor errors like double-scanning items.

## Business Model and Financial Implications
- Costco's retention strategy contributes to its 20-year revenue growth and 2,000% stock price increase since 2008.
- The company's low employee turnover (7% vs. industry average of ~20%) reduces onboarding costs and improves service consistency.
- The company's 401(k) plan, transitioned from a pension to a defined-contribution plan in 1993, enables long-term wealth accumulation for employees.
- Costco's model demonstrates that investing in employee well-being can yield long-term financial and operational returns.

## Key Takeaways
- High employee retention leads to better customer experiences and lower operational costs.
- Investing in employee well-being—especially healthcare, mental health, and career development—can significantly improve retention and productivity.
- Experienced employees are not just cost centers; they are cultural assets that enhance service quality and brand loyalty.
- A company's retention strategy directly impacts its bottom line through reduced training costs and improved customer satisfaction.
