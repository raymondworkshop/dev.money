# [Don't become an Engineering Manager](https://newsletter.manager.dev/p/dont-become-an-engineering-manager)

## Core View
- AI coding tools are fast, capable, and context-blind; they miss team conventions and past decisions, leading to costly rework and token waste.
- [AI Synthesis] Unblocked improves organizational context by integrating code, PR history, and runtime signals, enabling agents to operate with engineering-level understanding.
- [AI Synthesis] The pace of change in software engineering has accelerated dramatically, making it difficult to maintain continuity in team workflows and project design.
- [AI Synthesis] The engineering management ladder is now highly competitive and flattened, with fewer Director and VP roles available, and promotion often requires managing more engineers — a barrier in current market conditions.
- [AI Synthesis] Compensation for engineering managers is often lower than for Senior/Staff Engineers, especially when comparing across startups and industries.
- [AI Synthesis] The article recommends against transitioning to management in 2026 due to time constraints, lack of growth opportunities, and lower pay — but acknowledges that personal passion for the role may justify the move.

## Key Takeaways
- Engineering managers face significant tradeoffs in 2026 due to rapid technological change and a flattened career ladder.
- AI tools are increasingly capable but lack contextual awareness, making them less effective than human-in-the-loop systems.
- Staying as a senior engineer offers better financial returns and more autonomy in innovation.
- Personal enjoyment of the work is a key factor in deciding whether to pursue engineering management.
