# [China Releases Imprisoned Pastor Ezra Jin After U.S. Pressure](https://cn.wsj.com/articles/china-releases-imprisoned-pastor-ezra-jin-after-u-s-pressure-c4a82eef?mod=cn_hp_lead_pos9)

## Core View
- Ezra Jin, a prominent leader of the underground Zion Church, was released after nearly nine months in detention following sustained U.S. diplomatic pressure.
- Jin arrived in Los Angeles on July 6, 2026, just one day before the U.S. 250th anniversary, and has since reunited with his wife, Liu Chunli, and daughter Grace Jin Drexel.
- The release occurred amid broader efforts to stabilize U.S.-China relations, with both leaders expressing optimism about bilateral ties after the May summit.
- Jin's detention was linked to his leadership of an unauthorized Christian congregation, which operated in secret and later transitioned to online operations using Zoom and similar tools.

## Background and Context
- Jin founded the Zion Church in Beijing, initially operating in a former nightclub, and expanded its reach through digital platforms after the 2018 crackdown.
- In 2018, Chinese authorities shut down the physical church and began a campaign to suppress unauthorized religious activities.
- The release of Jin and nine other members of the Zion Church occurred in recent weeks, with the family and ChinaAid reporting that the latter group had been granted bail.
- Jin's arrest and detention drew condemnation from U.S. lawmakers and the public, with President Trump publicly urging President Xi to consider the release.

## Human and Social Impact
- Jin's daughter, Grace Jin Drexel, is a U.S. citizen and had been actively advocating for her father's release.
- His wife, Liu Chunli, had previously relocated to the U.S. due to safety concerns and has welcomed him back.
- The family expressed gratitude to the U.S. government and requested public prayer during this period of transition.

## Key Takeaways
- The release of Pastor Ezra Jin signals a potential shift in China's handling of religious freedom under international pressure.
- This event underscores the role of religious freedom as a diplomatic lever in U.S.-China relations.
- It highlights the resilience of underground religious movements and their adaptation through digital platforms.
- The case raises questions about the long-term implications for religious expression and civil liberties in China.
