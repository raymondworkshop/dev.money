# [Forget Working: Passive Income Is the New American Dream](https://cn.wsj.com/articles/passive-income-dreams-f4ec7dda?mod=cn_hp_lead_pos10)

## Core View
- Greg Keogh, a 30-something man from Austin, Texas, found success by designing a narrow, high-demand vacuum cleaner for pet hair, which sold well on Amazon and now generates $50,000-$115,000 annually with minimal effort.
- The article highlights a growing trend where individuals are seeking passive income as a form of personal liberation, with 44% of 18-28 year olds reporting side income sources.
- Platforms like Airbnb, Turo, and Etsy are enabling users to monetize underutilized assets such as cars, homes, and personal skills.
- AI tools are accelerating the rise of passive income models, enabling users to generate content (e.g., audio, videos, PDFs) at scale and monetize it through platforms like ElevenLabs and Etsy.
- However, many so-called 'passive income' schemes are scams—FTC has shut down several operations for defrauding consumers of millions in fees and investments.

## Case Studies of Passive Income Models
- Michael Tremblay, a paper mill worker, earns hundreds of dollars monthly by generating AI-generated PDF guides and workbooks for Etsy, targeting niche audiences (e.g., ADHD women who love hiking).
- Matt Ebso, a 31-year-old from the U.S., created a library of AI-generated voice clones for use in audiobooks and meditation, earning about $3,000/month.
- Anna Lohrmann, a former Spanish teacher, tried multiple courses and failed to profit, illustrating the high failure rate of 'get-rich-quick' schemes. She notes AI often gives overly optimistic projections (e.g., $7,000/year for a Spanish test suite, which only brought in $250).
- Ronnie Lim, a 19-year-old from Georgia, earns thousands monthly by reselling Amazon products on eBay, using a network of proxy accounts to avoid platform restrictions.

## Risks and Critiques
- [AI Synthesis] The article identifies a significant risk: many passive income models are not truly passive and require ongoing effort to maintain, update, or scale. For example, Greg Keogh initially spent significant time fixing defective parts before achieving true 'passivity'.
- [AI Synthesis] The 'passive income' trend is often misaligned with reality—many individuals are working harder to achieve it, and the 'freedom' is often illusory, as highlighted by the failure of numerous online courses and digital products.
- [AI Synthesis] Regulatory and legal risks exist—eBay and Amazon prohibit reselling items from their platforms, and cases like the North Carolina man who used AI to generate millions of fake songs and manipulate metrics show the potential for fraud and legal consequences.

## Broader Cultural and Economic Shifts
- A 2023 Bankrate survey found that 25% of Americans have a side job, and 60% of Z-generation adults believe traditional full-time work cannot meet their financial goals.
- Search trends on Google and Reddit show a 50% increase in passive income queries since the 2020s, with weekly traffic to dedicated subreddits reaching 500,000 visitors.
- The concept of 'passive income' is increasingly being used as a lifestyle ideal—work is no longer a necessity, and time is now a valuable asset.

## Key Takeaways
- Passive income is no longer just a financial strategy—it's a cultural and personal lifestyle shift.
- Many so-called passive income models are not truly passive and require significant effort to maintain or scale.
- AI tools are enabling new, scalable models (e.g., voice cloning, content generation), but also increasing the risk of scams and overpromising.
- The trend reflects a broader societal disillusionment with traditional employment and a desire for autonomy and time freedom.
