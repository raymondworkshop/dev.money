# [Wayve's AI Car Drives London Streets, Challenging Tesla and Waymo](https://cn.wsj.com/articles/wayve-self-driving-technology-984fa671?mod=cn_hp_lead_pos11)

## Core View
- Wayve, a UK-based AI startup, is developing an end-to-end AI system for autonomous driving that competes with Tesla and Waymo.
- The technology has been tested in a Jeep Grand Cherokee, with Stellantis engineers reporting smooth navigation through busy urban environments, including traffic signals and parking signs.
- Wayve's approach uses only cameras and radar, avoiding expensive lidar sensors used by most competitors.
- In 2024, Wayve raised $10.5 billion, led by SoftBank, with additional funding from Microsoft, NVIDIA, Uber, and automotive giants including Stellantis, Mercedes-Benz, and Nissan.
- Wayve plans to launch autonomous taxi services in London with Uber by summer 2026, followed by expansion into U.S. cities.
- The company's AI system learns from vast datasets of driving videos, similar to ChatGPT, enabling it to generalize across diverse scenarios without relying on pre-mapped high-definition city maps.

## Technology and Innovation
- Wayve's 'end-to-end' AI model processes raw sensor input directly into driving decisions, eliminating the need for rule-based systems and traditional mapping.
- This approach contrasts with hybrid systems (e.g., Waymo, Apollo Go) that combine AI with high-precision maps and safety rules.
- Critics argue that end-to-end AI lacks transparency and regulatory compliance, while proponents claim it offers better generalization and real-world adaptability.
- Wayve has conducted field tests in 506 cities, demonstrating robust performance in diverse and unpredictable urban conditions.

## Partnerships and Commercialization
- Stellantis has committed to integrating Wayve's AI driving system across its brands, including Chrysler, Dodge, and Ram, starting in 2028.
- Nissan has also adopted the technology, with potential early deployment in Japan.
- Wayve is in talks with most of the top 10 automakers globally, positioning itself as a scalable, off-the-shelf solution for mass-market adoption.
- Future production vehicles will include 10 cameras and 5 radars, improving data input for AI decision-making while maintaining a more compact, less intrusive design than traditional systems.

## Key Differentiators
- No reliance on expensive lidar — reduces hardware cost and complexity for OEMs.
- London-based development allows Wayve to maintain a distinct, non-Silicon Valley-aligned technical path.
- The system is designed to operate in both highway and city environments, with human oversight required during early deployment phases.

## Key Takeaways
- Wayve's end-to-end AI system offers a scalable, cost-efficient alternative to traditional rule-based autonomous driving systems.
- Partnerships with major automakers like Stellantis and Nissan signal strong industry confidence in the technology's reliability and safety.
- The absence of lidar and reliance on visual sensors may reduce deployment barriers for traditional OEMs seeking to enter the autonomous driving market.
