# [Nokia's New Chapter: Becoming a Supplier of AI Data Center Infrastructure](https://cn.wsj.com/articles/nokias-new-act-supplying-the-ai-data-center-boom-60137bff?mod=cn_hp_featst_pos2)

## Core View
- Nokia, under CEO Justin Hotard, has pivoted from mobile phones to supplying critical AI infrastructure, including optical switches and routers that manage data flow in data centers.
- Nokia's products function as the 'logistics' of AI data centers—transferring data between servers, analogous to trucks and sorting hubs in a delivery network.
- The company's optical network division grew significantly, with market share rising from 6.3% to 27.3% in North America, placing it second behind Ciena (50.1% share).
- Nokia acquired Infinera for $2.3 billion, enhancing its capabilities in optical transport technologies, including lasers, receivers, and chips.
- Nokia's AI infrastructure revenue grew strongly in Q1, leading to a year-over-year growth forecast of 18–20%, up from prior expectations.

## Strategic Shift and Market Position
- Nokia's 1865 founding as a pulp mill and subsequent evolution into rubber, cables, and mobile phones shows a long history of industrial transformation.
- The company sold its mobile phone business to Microsoft in 2014 and now licenses the brand to third-party manufacturers.
- Its current mobile infrastructure segment accounts for over half of total revenue but is declining due to completed 5G network rollouts.

## Partnerships and Investment Highlights
- Nvidia acquired 2.9% of Nokia's shares for $100 million as part of a strategic product collaboration, reinforcing supply chain and technological alignment.
- Nokia's leadership team now includes key executives from Silicon Valley, accelerating AI infrastructure development.

## Challenges and Risks
- [AI Synthesis] Supply chain constraints are a key risk—Nokia faces shortages in key optical components due to intense competition and long lead times.
- [AI Synthesis] The company's reliance on semiconductor supply chains introduces exposure to price volatility and supply unpredictability, which could impact revenue predictability.
- [AI Synthesis] Market sentiment remains volatile: investors are concerned about the company's ability to achieve AI-sector profitability, with current valuations comparable to AI startups but no proven margins yet.

## Key Takeaways
- Nokia is transitioning from a mobile device manufacturer to a key supplier of AI data center infrastructure, particularly in optical networking.
- The company's market share in North American optical networks has expanded significantly, reaching 27.3%.
- Despite strong revenue growth, the business faces supply chain risks and investor skepticism about profitability in the AI infrastructure space.
- Strategic partnerships with Nvidia and a new leadership focused on AI infrastructure are accelerating its market positioning.
